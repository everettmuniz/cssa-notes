# November 8 — The Baptism and Temptation of Jesus

**Aim:** Learn about Jesus and let his example shape the people we are becoming.

## Introduction

I'm looking forward to spending time with you learning about and *from* the life of Jesus. We don't just want to get to know what he did, what mattered to him, and how he treated people. We also want to understand what that tells about God wants all of his children to think and live.

Before we get started this morning I want to read two short passages together:

- [2 Timothy 3:15–17](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2+Timothy+3%3A15-17&version=KJV): Scripture leads us toward salvation through faith in Christ. It teaches, corrects, and trains us so that we are equipped to do good. God is preparing us for something we will actually do.
- [Romans 12:1–2](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Romans+12%3A1-2&version=KJV): In response to God's mercy, we offer our lives to him. We are to be transformed through renewed minds, learning to recognize and choose his will. As we listen to God's word, we want it to change the way we think.

**What I want you to notice is that the goal of studying the Bible isn't just to know things; it's to become a new kind of person.** As we learn about Jesus together over the next few months, the challenge is to use what we learn to to become more like him.*

### How class will work each Sunday

1. **Warm up:** Share a recent win or highlight, a current struggle or challenge, and something we're looking forward to.
2. **Read the Scripture around:** Take turns reading and make sure we understand what the passage says.
3. **Read the lesson around:** Take turns reading and make sure we understand what the lesson says.
4. **Lessons for us:** Discuss why these things matter, what they show us about God and Jesus, and how they can change our lives.

During the two readings, start with understanding the words and following the account. Keep the bigger questions about why, meaning, and application for the discussion. Jot those questions down so we can return to them.

## Lessons for us

The first three discussions develop the ideas in the November 7 planning transcript. The fourth draws out the lesson's emphasis on overcoming temptation. Use the prompts that help the conversation; they aren't a checklist to finish.

### 1. Learn to notice where God is working

**Read [Luke 3:1–2](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke+3%3A1-2&version=KJV)** as a short supplement to Matthew's account. Luke names an emperor, governors, rulers, and high priests, then tells us that God's word came to John in the wilderness.

The people with the most attention and influence aren't necessarily the people who help us see what God is doing. God was preparing people for Jesus through John. Fame doesn't tell us how much someone's life matters to God's purpose.

**Discuss:**

- If you were living then, whose activities might have seemed most important? What does Luke draw our attention to?
- Sports, politics, and entertainment receive a lot of attention today. How can that affect what we think matters?
- Where could we pay more attention to someone quietly serving God, or to an opportunity to serve him ourselves?

**Bring it home:** We don't need to be famous to take part in God's work. Pay attention to God and to the people and opportunities he has put near us.

### 2. Obedience makes us available to God's purpose

**Matthew 3:13–17**, especially Jesus' answer in verse 15. The lesson emphasizes his willingness to do what was right before God, even though he was sinless.

There's something deeper about Jesus' obedience than simply that he did what God told him to do. Jesus understood that God's commands have a purpose. 

Through obedience...

* we honor God and show our love for him,
	* when you pay attention and respond to someone, you show them you care about them
* we learn to care about what he cares about, and 
	* God's commands reflect how he thinks and feels, so our obedience gives us the opportunity to learn to think and feel like he does
* we make ourselves available for him to work through us
	* God is working a plan to set the world right and obedience allows us to be part of that work. It begins to set things right in us, which makes us useful to God's work of setting things right around us.

> [!NOTE]
> We may see how a choice changes us without seeing all the ways God uses it to help others. Understanding the whole outcome isn't a prerequisite for trusting him.

**Discuss:**

- What does Jesus' willingness to be baptized show us about his attitude toward his Father?
- How could telling the truth, keeping a promise, or including someone shape us and also help someone else?
- What can help us choose obedience when we can't see what good will come from it?

**Bring it home:** Choose one ordinary act of obedience this week. Consider what it expresses about our love for God and the kind of person he is helping us become.

### 3. Faithful living includes repentance and forgiveness

**Return to Matthew 3:6–8** and the lesson's call to repentance. When the lesson speaks about obeying God's commands with faith and love, remember the transcript's point: acknowledging sin and seeking forgiveness belong within that response to God.

**Read [1 John 1:8–2:2](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1+John+1%3A8-2%3A2&version=KJV).** John calls us to avoid sin, to be honest about our failures, and to seek the forgiveness God provides through Jesus. Faithfulness doesn't mean claiming we have lived flawlessly. It means continuing to turn toward God, confessing when we fall short, and seeking his help to change.

**Discuss:**

- What is the difference between making excuses for a wrong choice and repenting of it?
- Suppose someone joins in mocking a classmate. What could confession, making amends, and a changed response look like?
- How does knowing that God provides forgiveness help us be honest and keep trying?

**Bring it home:** When we fail, acknowledge it, ask forgiveness, put things right where we can, and ask for help to act differently. Our hope rests in God's mercy through Jesus as we learn to follow him, not on flawless obedience.

### 4. Let God's word shape our response to temptation

**Matthew 4:1–11.** The CSSA lesson connects Jesus' replies with Deuteronomy 8:3, 6:16, and 6:13. He brought God's word to bear on the choices in front of him.

- **Stones into bread:** A real need doesn't make every way of meeting it right. Learn to trust God when we feel pressure to satisfy ourselves immediately.
- **Jump from the temple:** Trusting God doesn't mean creating a crisis to make him prove that he cares for us.
- **The kingdoms of the world:** Something attractive or powerful isn't worth turning away from God to obtain.

The tempter also quoted Scripture. Jesus' response reminds us to understand God's word in context and let it direct our choices. Knowing a verse becomes useful as we learn to live by it.

**Discuss:**

- When might we be tempted to do something wrong because we want something now, want to impress people, or want to get ahead?
- Choose one example. What truth from today's reading could help us respond differently?
- What could help us remember that truth before the moment becomes difficult?

**Encouragement:** [Hebrews 4:15–16](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Hebrews+4%3A15-16&version=KJV) tells us that Jesus understands temptation and directs us to seek mercy and help. We can ask for God's help while learning from Jesus' example.
