# Jesus Meets Nicodemus the Pharisee

**Sunday, November 15, 2026 — Intermediate, Stage 4, Lesson 2**

**Aim:** Learn about Jesus and let his example shape the people we are becoming.

## Introduction

Last week's lesson introduced Jesus' willingness to obey his Father and let God's word guide his choices. This week we see what mattered to him when he came to the Temple, and what he wanted Nicodemus to understand about entering God's Kingdom.

Nicodemus already knew a great deal about religion. He recognized that God was working through Jesus, but he still had things to learn. As we read, think about what it means to let God change the way we think and live. This picks up on the transformation theme described in [[11_08#Introduction]].

We'll follow the same pattern as last week: warm up, read the Scripture around, read the lesson around, and then discuss lessons for us. During the readings, start with understanding the words and following the account. Jot down bigger questions about meaning and application so we can return to them in the discussion.

Take turns reading a few verses each. Pause at these natural breaks:

- **John 3:11–17:** The Son of man being lifted up, belief, and God's love for the world.
- **John 3:18–21:** Belief and unbelief, darkness and light.

**Focus: What does the passage say?** Use these as needed:

- What does Jesus find in the Temple? What does he do and say?
- What do the people think Jesus means by destroying and raising the Temple? What does John explain that he means?
- Who is Nicodemus, and what does he say he knows about Jesus?
- How does Nicodemus understand being born again? How does Jesus explain further?
- Which event from Moses' time does Jesus mention?
- What does the passage say God has done because he loves the world? What response does it call for?
- What reasons are given for avoiding the light or coming to it?

Clarify unfamiliar words such as *Passover*, *zeal*, *Pharisee*, *Rabbi*, and *condemn*. Ask students to point to the verse that supports an answer. Save the reasons behind people's choices and the connections to our lives for later.

## Lessons for us

Use the prompts that help the conversation; they aren't a checklist to finish. Keep returning to what the passages show us about God and Jesus, and how we can respond.

### 1. Care about what honors God

**Return to John 2:13–17.** Jesus calls the Temple his Father's house. What was happening there mattered to him because he cared about his Father and how people worshipped him.

**Some context:** Worshippers often traveled long distances to Jerusalem. Buying suitable sacrificial animals there spared them the difficulty of bringing animals from home. Money changers helped visitors exchange their currency for money accepted for Temple payments. These services met real needs.

The [background article on animal sellers and money changers](https://morethansundaymornings.com/2026/02/24/why-were-they-selling-animals-in-the-temple/) places the trading in the outer court, commonly called the Court of the Gentiles. This was the area accessible to non-Jewish worshippers. Filling it with animals, bargaining, and noise could crowd out people who had come to seek God.

John 2:16 explicitly identifies turning God's house into a marketplace as the problem. The CSSA lesson also describes greed and exploitation; John does not give prices or exchange rates. Consider how the arrangements for worship affected the people coming to worship.

Jesus' concern gives us something to examine in ourselves. When we come together to worship, our choices can help other people listen, pray, and take part. They also express how much God's place in our lives matters to us.

**Discuss:**

- What does Jesus' response tell us about what mattered to him?
- How did animal sellers and money changers meet the needs of people traveling to Jerusalem?
- How could those services end up making worship harder, especially for people with the least access?
- Suppose a friend is trying to listen or a visitor feels unsure about joining in. How could our behavior help them?
- How might we honor God through the way we treat people during the rest of the week?

**Bring it home:** Choose one way to give God your attention and help someone else take part in worship this week.

### 2. Let God's word change our hearts and direction

**Return to John 3:1–10.** Nicodemus was a ruler and a teacher, yet Jesus told him he needed to be born again. His position and knowledge did not remove his need for the new life God was offering.

**Read [1 Peter 1:22–23](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1+Peter+1%3A22-23&version=KJV).** Peter connects being born again through God's word with obedience to the truth and sincere love for one another. We can look for the effect of God's word in the way we treat people.

The CSSA lesson explains this as a change of mind and heart. It connects being born of water with baptism, when a believer commits to leaving the old life and living in obedience to God. It connects being born of the Spirit with God's future gift of immortality in the Kingdom. God works in us now through his word, and we depend on him for the lasting change to our nature that we cannot bring about ourselves.

**If clarification is needed:** [Romans 6:3–4](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Romans+6%3A3-4&version=KJV) connects baptism with beginning a new life; [1 Corinthians 15:50–54](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1+Corinthians+15%3A50-54&version=KJV) describes the change from mortality to immortality.

**Discuss:**

- Why might it be hard for someone who already knows a lot to accept that they still need to change?
- What is something we could know from the Bible that needs to affect our behavior? What would that look like in practice?
- Peter mentions sincere love. How could God's word change the way we respond to someone who annoys us or is left out?
- What question about baptism or the new life would you like to understand better?

**Bring it home:** Choose one teaching from God's word and one way to act on it this week. Ask God to help it become part of the way you think and live.

### 3. Respond to God's love with trust

**Read [Numbers 21:4–9](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Numbers+21%3A4-9&version=KJV), then return to John 3:14–17.** The Israelites had sinned, and the people bitten by serpents could not cure themselves. They confessed their sin, and God provided a way for them to live. Those who were bitten had to trust his provision and look at the bronze serpent Moses raised.

Jesus used that event to help Nicodemus understand what would happen through him. He would be lifted up in crucifixion, and those who believe in him could receive eternal life. John 3:16–17 puts God's love at the center of this: God gave his Son so that people could be saved.

This builds on last week's discussion about repentance and forgiveness. We need God's mercy and the salvation he provides through Jesus. Trusting that love gives us a reason to turn toward him, ask for forgiveness, and learn to follow his Son.

**Discuss:**

- What did the Israelites need God to do for them? How did they respond to the way he provided?
- What does John 3:16–17 show us about what God wants for people?
- How could remembering God's love help someone who feels ashamed of a wrong choice come to him for help?
- How should God's love for the world affect the way we treat people from a different background from ours?

**Bring it home:** When you pray this week, thank God for giving his Son. Bring him something you need forgiveness or help with, trusting his willingness to save.

### 4. Keep learning, and let faith grow into courage

**Return to John 3:19–21.** Coming to the light means being willing for the truth to expose what needs to change. That can be uncomfortable, especially when we care about what other people think of us.

Nicodemus did not understand everything during this first conversation, but this is not the last we hear of him. **Read [John 7:45–52](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John+7%3A45-52&version=KJV) and [John 19:38–42](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John+19%3A38-42&version=KJV).** He later questioned whether the rulers were treating Jesus fairly, and he helped Joseph of Arimathea prepare Jesus' body for burial.

These later accounts give us more to notice about Nicodemus than his initial confusion. We can keep asking honest questions, listening to Jesus, and taking the next faithful step as our understanding grows.

**Discuss:**

- What can we learn from the fact that a respected teacher had questions he could not answer?
- What did Nicodemus risk by questioning the way the other rulers were acting? How did they respond?
- Suppose friends are mocking someone or repeating an unfair story. What might a small, courageous response look like?
- Is there a question you could ask, a wrong you could acknowledge, or a kind action you could take even if it feels awkward?

**Bring it home:** Take one step toward the light this week: ask an honest question, admit something that needs to change, or speak and act fairly when there is pressure to go along with others.
## Closing

Return to the purpose of the class: learning from Jesus should equip us to live differently.

Invite each person to finish: **“One thing I want to remember or practice this week is…”** They can keep their answer private if they prefer.

Close with a prayer thanking God for his love in giving Jesus, asking him to change our hearts through his word and help us honor him in our choices.
