# Belief in the Messiah at Samaria

**Sunday, November 22, 2026 — Intermediate, Stage 4, Lesson 3**

**Aim:** Learn about Jesus and let his example shape the people we are becoming.

## Introduction

Last week a respected teacher of Israel came to Jesus by night. This week Jesus starts a conversation with a Samaritan woman at a well, probably around midday. Nicodemus knew a great deal and still had things to learn. This woman belonged to a people the Jews avoided, and yet she was the one who went and told her whole town about Jesus.

As we read, notice who Jesus is willing to talk to, how he moves the conversation from ordinary water to eternal life, and what he says true worship looks like. Last week we read that God loved the world and sent his Son (John 3:16–17). Today we see some of what that looks like in practice ([[11_15#3. Respond to God's love with trust]]).

We'll follow the same pattern as the last two weeks: warm up, read the Scripture around, read the lesson around, and then discuss lessons for us. During the readings, start with understanding the words and following the account. Jot down bigger questions about meaning and application so we can return to them in the discussion.

## Read the Scripture around

**Main reading: John 4:1–42.** This is the passage the lesson names. [Read online](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John+4%3A1-42&version=KJV).

Take turns reading a few verses each. Pause at these natural breaks:

- **John 4:1–6:** Jesus leaves Judea for Galilee, passes through Samaria, and rests at Jacob's well near Sychar. [Map of the provinces](../books/intermediate_4_graphics/page-018.png)
- **John 4:7–15:** Jesus asks the woman for a drink and offers her living water.
- **John 4:16–26:** Her husbands, the question about where to worship, and Jesus telling her he is the Messiah.
- **John 4:27–38:** The disciples return, the woman goes into the city, and Jesus talks about his food and the harvest.
- **John 4:39–42:** Many Samaritans believe and ask Jesus to stay.

**Focus: What does the passage say?** Use these as needed:

- Why does Jesus leave Judea? Which way does he go, and where does he stop? (vv. 1–6)
- Why is the woman surprised that Jesus asks her for a drink? (v. 9)
- How does Jesus describe the water he can give? How does the woman understand him? (vv. 10–15)
- What does Jesus know about her life? What does she conclude about him? (vv. 16–19)
- What question does she ask about worship? What does Jesus say about the Father and true worshippers? (vv. 20–24)
- What does Jesus tell her about himself? (vv. 25–26)
- What surprises the disciples when they return? What does the woman do and say in the city? (vv. 27–30)
- What does Jesus say his food is? What does he tell the disciples about the harvest? (vv. 31–38)
- Why did the Samaritans believe? What do they say about Jesus at the end? (vv. 39–42)

Clarify unfamiliar words and phrases such as *Samaria*, *Sychar*, *parcel of ground*, *the sixth hour*, *this mountain*, *Messias*, and *reap*. Ask students to point to the verse that supports an answer. Save the reasons behind people's choices and the connections to our lives for later.

**If background is needed:** [2 Kings 17:24–33](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2+Kings+17%3A24-33&version=KJV) describes how the king of Assyria settled other peoples in Samaria after Israel was taken captive, and how their worship became a mixture: “They feared the LORD, and served their own gods” (v. 33).

**Focus: What does the lesson say?**

- Who does it say the Samaritans were, and why were Jews and Samaritans hostile to each other?

Keep these answers descriptive: “The lesson says…” Note questions or claims we want to examine during the discussion. The reference library and end questions can be used as follow-up rather than read aloud.

**Teacher's notes:**

- **Who baptized:** The lesson says Jesus was baptizing. John 4:2 adds that “Jesus himself baptized not, but his disciples.”
- **Why Jesus left Judea:** John 4:1–3 says Jesus knew the Pharisees had heard about his growing number of disciples, but it doesn't give his reason for leaving. The lesson's “hateful Pharisees” goes beyond the text. Also, the lesson says he “chose” the route through Samaria; John 4:4 says he “must needs go through Samaria.”
- **Date:** Samaria fell to Assyria about 722 BC (2 Kings 17:6), not “about 750 years B.C.”
- **The woman's history:** John 4:17–18 tells us she had had five husbands and that the man she was with wasn't her husband. It doesn't tell us why. Some of her marriages may have ended in death or divorce. Be careful about filling in the details.
- **Gerizim:** The lesson says destruction would come on Gerizim and Jerusalem about 40 years later. The Temple in Jerusalem was destroyed in AD 70. The Samaritan temple on Mount Gerizim, however, had already been destroyed by a Jewish ruler more than a century before Jesus spoke, although Samaritans kept worshipping on the mountain. In John 4:21 Jesus' main point is that true worship would no longer depend on either place.
- **“God is a Spirit” (v. 24):** The lesson explains this as God being a living, intelligent being, not a lifeless idol. We can add that God is the one God, the Father (v. 23; John 17:3), who “only hath immortality” (1 Timothy 6:16). Worshipping “in spirit” describes the worshipper's mind and heart, i.e., sincerity and not outward show. It isn't about receiving a special gift before we can worship.
- **“First public announcement”:** Jesus said this to one woman, not to a crowd. It's the first time in John's Gospel that Jesus himself says plainly that he is the Messiah, but it was a private conversation.
- **Section heading:** The heading “Jn. 4:26-35” covers material to the end of the account (v. 42).

### 1. Jesus talked with people others avoided

**Return to John 4:7–9 and 4:27.** The woman was surprised that a Jew would ask her for a drink. The disciples “marvelled that he talked with the woman,” although none of them questioned him. Jesus knew the history between Jews and Samaritans. He still began the conversation, and he treated her with respect.

This builds on last week's point that God loved the world (John 3:16). At the end of today's reading, the Samaritans call Jesus “the Saviour of the world” (v. 42).

**Discuss:**

- What lines did Jesus cross by talking with this woman?
- Why do you think the disciples didn't ask him about it?
- Who tends to get left out or avoided at school, at work, or even at the ecclesia? Why?
- What makes it hard to start a conversation with someone outside our usual group?

**Bring it home:** Start one conversation this week with someone you'd normally overlook. Ask them a question and listen to the answer.

### 2. What we thirst for, and where to find it

**Return to John 4:13–15.** Jesus contrasts water that leaves us thirsty again with water that becomes “a well of water springing up into everlasting life.” The woman is thinking about not having to come to the well every day. Jesus is talking about the life God offers.

The lesson connects living water with God's word. **Read [Isaiah 55:1–3](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Isaiah+55%3A1-3&version=KJV)** and [John 6:63](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John+6%3A63&version=KJV): “the words that I speak unto you, they are spirit, and they are life.”

**Discuss:**

- What does Isaiah say about spending money on what doesn't satisfy?
- What do people our age keep going back to, hoping it will satisfy them (approval, likes, buying things, winning)? Why doesn't it last?
- How is God's word different from those things?
- What could help us actually come back to God's word during the week, not just on Sunday?

**Bring it home:** Choose a short passage to read each day this week, e.g. a few verses of John. Ask what it shows about God or Jesus.

### 3. True worship is a matter of the heart and the truth

**Return to John 4:19–24.** The woman raises an old argument about the right place to worship. Jesus answers it: salvation is of the Jews, because God made his promises to Israel (v. 22). But he moves the conversation from *where* to *how*. The Father seeks people who worship him “in spirit and in truth.”

**Read [Joshua 24:14](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Joshua+24%3A14&version=KJV).** Near this same place, Joshua told Israel to “serve him in sincerity and in truth” and to put away other gods. We need both. Sincerity without the truth can be wrong, and knowing the truth without sincerity can turn into a show.

This picks up last week's point about caring about what honors God ([[11_15#1. Care about what honors God]]).

**Discuss:**

- What was the woman's question about? What did Jesus think mattered more?
- What might worshipping God “in spirit” look like for us? What might it look like to worship “in truth”?
- Can someone go through the right motions without really worshipping God? How would they know?
- What helps you pay attention to God during the memorial service, prayer, or the readings?

**Bring it home:** Before the memorial service or your own prayers this week, take a moment to think about who you are speaking to and what you want to say to Him.

### 4. Tell others what you've found

**Return to John 4:28–30 and 4:39–42.** The woman left her waterpot and went into the city. She didn't have all the answers: “Come, see a man, which told me all things that ever I did: is not this the Christ?” Many believed because of what she said. Many more believed when they heard Jesus for themselves.

Jesus told the disciples the fields were already “white to harvest” (v. 35). People were ready to listen.

**Discuss:**

- What did the woman actually say to the people in the city? Why do you think it worked?
- What can we learn from the fact that she shared before she understood everything?
- What would make it hard for you to tell a friend something you've learned about Jesus?
- What's one simple thing you could say or invite someone to?

**Bring it home:** Tell one person something you've learned about Jesus this month, or invite them to come and see.

## Closing

Return to the purpose of the class: learning from Jesus should equip us to live differently.

Invite each person to finish: **“One thing I want to remember or practice this week is…”** They can keep their answer private if they prefer.

Close with a prayer thanking God that He seeks true worshippers, asking Him to help us come to His word for life and to welcome people others avoid.
